** ⋆. 𐙚 ˚ Final Project (Updated January 2026) ⋆. 𐙚 ˚** 

A Bayesian re-analysis of phonological indeterminacy in L2 learners, replicating Ota et al. (2009) with modern statistical methods.

** ⋆. 𐙚 ˚ Key Findings ⋆. 𐙚 ˚** 
1. L1-Absent contrasts (/l/-/r/) produce 21% error rates—functionally equivalent to true homophones
2. Phonological distinctness acts as a **gradient** constraint, not a binary deficit
3. The effect generalizes across all lexical items (not driven by outliers)

**⋆. 𐙚 ˚ Overview ⋆. 𐙚 ˚** 

This course introduces the fundamentals of statistics and quantitative data analysis for linguistics. Students will learn how to design robust studies that address meaningful research questions. Drawing from various branches of linguistics, the curriculum emphasizes open scholarship and research integrity.

**⋆. 𐙚 ˚Course Highlights ⋆. 𐙚 ˚**
- Foundations of statistical analysis and data visualization
- Principles of quantitative research methodology
- Hands-on applications using R and RStudio
- Practical approaches to summarizing, visualizing, and modeling linguistic data

**⋆. 𐙚 ˚ Core Skills Developed ⋆. 𐙚 ˚**
- Rigorous study design and data analysis
- Experience in open, inclusive, and diverse research practices
- Statistical inference using linear models
- Interpretation of p-values and Bayesian statistical results

**⋆. 𐙚 ˚Approach⋆. 𐙚 ˚**

The course emphasizes general principles of data analysis and fosters an egalitarian and inclusive scholarly environment. Students will develop the technical and conceptual skills necessary for contemporary linguistic research using open-source tools.

**⋆. 𐙚 ˚Learning Outcomes⋆. 𐙚 ˚**
- Upon completion of this course, students will be able to:
- Conduct data analysis using R and RStudio
- Effectively communicate quantitative results
- Apply statistical inference to linguistic data, including linear and Bayesian models
